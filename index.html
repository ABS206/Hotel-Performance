Three connected measures

Occupancy (%) = rooms sold ÷ rooms available × 100

ADR = room revenue ÷ rooms sold

RevPAR = room revenue ÷ rooms available
= ADR × occupancy as a decimal

Keep full precision while calculating. Round only final answers to two decimals. If no rooms are sold, ADR is undefined; revenue and RevPAR may still be zero.

The national total, chain scale, and location categories describe different populations and may overlap. They are not four separate competing hotels. Select a benchmark and explain its limitations.

Occupancy gap is in percentage points. Dollar gaps are property minus benchmark. RevPAR index = property RevPAR ÷ benchmark RevPAR × 100; 100 means parity.

Discussion guide: avoid the occupancy trap

The luxury chain-scale category reports 73.6% occupancy versus 75.0% last year, while ADR rises from $425.49 to $444.89 and RevPAR from $319.11 to $327.35. Lower occupancy can coexist with higher RevPAR. Compare rate and volume together; these rounded figures do not establish a cause.

See the simulation model and limitations

Demand = baseline rooms × (proposed ADR ÷ baseline ADR)−sensitivity + additional room demand. Rooms sold = demand rounded to the nearest whole room, limited to 0–97. Excess demand is shown as unserved room requests.

Room revenue = rooms sold × ADR. Contribution = room revenue − occupied-room variable costs − marketing cost. This is not profit: fixed costs, taxes, commissions, displacement, and non-room revenue are excluded. Outreach adds demand without changing the rate mix. You set the assumptions; no actual market response is predicted.

Instructor facilitation & suggested assessment

Teams of 2–3: assign analyst, revenue manager, and presenter roles. Ask each team to save two trials for the same situation. Compare a discount strategy with a rate-protection strategy and discuss revenue versus contribution.

Suggested 10-point practice assessment: accurate calculations (3), benchmark interpretation (2), strategy evidence and tradeoffs (3), operations and marketing recommendations (2). This is a suggested practice rubric, separate from the Maplewood project rubric.

Extension: At 65 rooms sold at $150, a reduction to $135 needs at least 73 rooms sold to preserve room revenue. To preserve contribution with $35 variable cost and no marketing expense, it needs at least 75 rooms. Ask students to explain the difference.
